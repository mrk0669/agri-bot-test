AgriBot Architecture Plates

01 · System architecture

Computation splits across two tiers. The Jetson does perception and decision-making; the microcontroller does hard real-time actuation. They exchange one checksummed line protocol. The split is not only about throughput — because the MCU owns the actuators, it can stop the robot without the Jetson's help.

Plate 01 Complete system architecture — perception, decision, actuation, power Proposal Fig. 3

02 · Runtime tick order

One iteration of AgriBotRuntime.tick(). The ordering is the part worth understanding: each step is where it is because putting it elsewhere breaks something specific.

Plate 02 Control-loop tick — ordering and why each step sits where it does src/agribot/app/runtime.py

03 · Navigation pipeline

The rules require navigation by a computer-vision technique. A frame becomes a normalised error, the error becomes a wheel-speed differential. The one non-obvious stage is component selection, which exists because specular glare passes a "bright and unsaturated" threshold as convincingly as the line does.

Plate 03 Guidance-line extraction and the steering loop Proposal Fig. 4 · §5.2

04 · Perception and the crop veto

Three detector tiers run concurrently and are combined by a late-fusion rule. The rule is deliberately asymmetric: weed evidence accumulates, crop evidence vetoes. A false positive on a weed costs a little fluid; a false positive on a crop damages the plant the robot exists to protect.

Plate 04 Dual-path perception and the asymmetric late-fusion rule Proposal Fig. 7 · §5.6 · Novelty 1 & 4

05 · Kalman sensor fusion

Two independent filters, each fusing sources that fail in complementary ways. Row-end detection and spray spacing both depend on trustworthy odometry, and GPS is far too coarse at the centimetre scale required in-row.

Plate 05 Heading and distance filters — states, rates, and the innovation gate Proposal Fig. 5 · §5.3

06 · Mission state machine

The route is dictated by the crop rows, so the planner is not searching a graph for a shortest path — it manages a deterministic sequence of behaviours. Every transition is logged with its reason, which is what makes a run auditable afterwards.

Plate 06 Deterministic mission sequencer — all states and transitions Proposal Fig. 6 · §5.4

07 · Intervention sequence

Pixel coordinates become servo angles, and the burst runs as a non-blocking state machine so the ultrasonic safety layer and the MCU heartbeat stay alive while the nozzle is open.

Plate 07 Pixel-to-actuator mapping and the metered burst Proposal Fig. 9 · §5.7 · Novelty 2 & 3

08 · Safety layers

Three layers, deliberately independent. Two of them work with the Jetson absent, hung, or unplugged.

Plate 08 Independent safety layers and what each one survives firmware + mission FSM

Reproducing any number on these plates. Every quantity drawn here is produced by a command, not by hand: bash verify.sh runs all seven checks in order and exits non-zero if any claim stops holding.

One deviation from the published table. Plate 05 shows a fused heading RMSE of 0.27°; the proposal's Section 5.3 table says 0.60°. The implementation is better than the published figure because the magnetometer innovation gate sits at 8° and rejects the disturbance window outright. The prose claims — "stays within a degree", "a factor of roughly thirty" — remain true and are now conservative, but that one cell should read 0.27° and the factor "roughly seventy" if the document is to match the code.
